Search Wikipedia and retrieve the opening content of the best-matching article — up to approximately 1000 words of plain text, plus the article title and URL.

IMPORTANT: This tool matches articles by title, not by meaning. It will find the article whose title best matches your query — not the article that best answers your question. This means:
- Searching "What year did Einstein die?" will NOT find the answer. It will try to find an article titled that.
- Searching "Albert Einstein" WILL find the right article, which contains his death year.

Always call this tool before answering any factual question. Never answer from memory.

When to search again: if the retrieved article is irrelevant, try a different entity name. If the article is relevant but the specific fact is not in the first 1000 words, acknowledge this — do not guess or fill in from memory.

The Wikipedia article title or short topic phrase to search for. This should be a specific entity name or well-known concept — not a question or sentence.

Rules for good queries:
- Use the most specific, well-known name for the entity (e.g., "Marie Curie" not "famous female scientist")
- For events: use the event name ("French Revolution", "Battle of Hastings")
- For people: use their full common name ("Alexander Graham Bell", "Bong Joon-ho")
- For concepts: use the established Wikipedia title ("Theory of relativity", "Quantum mechanics")
- For disambiguation: add a clarifying word ("Mercury planet", "Mercury element", "Python programming language")

Good queries: "Marie Curie", "Battle of Hastings", "Python programming language", "2014 FIFA World Cup"
Bad queries: "What year did Marie Curie win the Nobel Prize?", "history of France", "who invented the telephone and where were they born"

For multi-step questions, search for the first entity in the chain, extract the intermediate fact from the result, then search the next entity using that fact.
